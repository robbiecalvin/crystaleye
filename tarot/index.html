03 / Come curious

A deck. A question. A different angle.

What is tarot?

Tarot is a deck of illustrated cards used for games, storytelling, and reflective reading. A typical modern deck has 78 cards: 22 Major Arcana and 56 Minor Arcana. Its documented roots lie in fifteenth-century European card games; later traditions developed the symbolic reading practices familiar today.

The Major Arcana explores broad themes and transitions. The Minor Arcana brings those questions into daily life through Swords, Cups, Pentacles, and Wands. A reading combines a question, a card’s imagery and meanings, and its position in a spread. It can open a useful conversation with yourself without making your future fixed.

How can I learn to read?

Start by noticing the image before looking up a meaning. Ask an open question such as “What deserves my attention?” Connect one detail in the card to your situation, then write down one realistic response. Study the Major Arcana story and each suit below, and use the Tarot Guide Book ($19.99 CAD) as a companion when ordering opens. Revisit your notes without forcing every event to fit a card.

Does a difficult card mean I will have a bad day?

No. An easy or hard label is a symbolic theme for reflection, not a reliable prediction. A demanding card can prompt you to slow down, seek help, or reconsider a pattern. Death speaks to endings and change; The Tower can question an unstable assumption. Neither establishes that harm is going to happen.
