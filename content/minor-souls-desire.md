## Overview and methodology

### A current-name lens on inner motivation

Minor Soul’s Desire, Minor Soul Urge, and Minor Heart’s Desire refer here to the current-name counterpart of the birth-name Soul’s Desire. Decoz explicitly derives it from the current name’s vowels and gives it the same calculation method. This guide reads it as a symbolic emphasis on everyday motivation and fulfillment. It is distinct from Minor Personality’s outward image and from Destiny / Expression’s capacities. It does not replace the main birth-name reading or describe a predetermined phase of life. [M, D0, N]

### Which name this project uses

Use the calculator’s current first, middle, and last-name inputs, including a middle name when supplied. If no alternative name is supplied, the birth-name inputs are reused, so main and minor results match. This is an input default, not a second independent confirmation. Decoz’s calculation overview specifies the current/social introduction name including surname, while the minor article often says shortened name. Bender’s adjacent Expression article describes minor names as current first and last. Record the exact name used rather than treating these scopes as interchangeable. [D0, M, BM; project app.js]

### Vowels and reduction order

To match the calculator, count A = 1, E = 5, I = 9, O = 6, and U = 3. Do not count Y or W as vowels. Sum vowel values within each whitespace-separated word, then add digits until the result is 1–9, 11, 22, or 33. Combine these reduced word totals and reduce with the same exceptions. Retain masters at completed word/final totals, not arbitrary running subtotals. A word with no counted vowels contributes 0. Apostrophes and hyphens carry no value. Decoz also uses name-part reduction, but his treatment of Y can differ. [D0, DY; project app.js]

### Worked example: Robert and Bobby

Invented birth name Robert James Smith: Robert has O(6) + E(5) = 11, retained; James has A(1) + E(5) = 6; Smith has I(9) = 9. Combine 11 + 6 + 9 = 26, then 8. Invented current name Bobby Smith: Bobby has O(6), and Smith has I(9); 6 + 9 = 15, then 6. Thus the project can show main Soul’s Desire 8 and Minor Soul’s Desire 6. These are arithmetic examples, not claims about an actual person. [Project app.js; editorial arithmetic]

### Retained results and a no-vowel case

The selected method retains 1–9, 11/2, 22/4, and 33/6. Decoz’s overview says main and minor calculations are the same and preserves all three masters. His dedicated Minor series provides only 1–9, 11, and 22; 33/6 is therefore included as an explicit adaptation from main Soul Urge sources, not as a quoted minor tradition. Roots do not rank human worth. Under the fixed-vowel project rule, Lynn Ng produces 0 because it has no A/E/I/O/U. No researched Minor Soul Urge 0 meaning was found: record no vowel-derived result to interpret, not an absence of desire. [D0, M, D33, B33; project app.js]

### Why vowel rules can change a result

Decoz treats Y as a vowel when it functions as a vowel sound and explicitly lists Lynn as an example; his rules do not assign W vowel status. Bender reports disagreement over whether Y is never, always, or conditionally included, and notes occasional W variants while excluding W in her own practice. Those reports are not independent readings of the books she cites. This guide keeps the calculator’s fixed A/E/I/O/U rule. Counting the Y in Lynn Ng as 7 instead would produce 7, not 0. Neither version should silently replace the declared convention. [DY, B0; editorial arithmetic]

### A word total is not the raw combined total

Decoz explicitly reduces each name part before combining vowel totals. The calculator follows that word-based approach. A raw-total method can change a master result. For the invented name John Amelia, John contributes O = 6; Amelia contributes A(1) + E(5) + I(9) + A(1) = 16, then 7. This guide produces 6 + 7 = 13, then 4. Combining raw vowel values gives 22 instead. Record the reduction order along with the name and vowel rule. [D0; project app.js; editorial arithmetic]

## Minor Soul’s Desire 1: Owning your everyday choices

Minor Soul's Desire 1 puts self-direction in the foreground of the current-name reading. You may find satisfaction in making a choice that feels personally authored, standing behind an idea, or recovering a sense of initiative within an established role. Decoz's minor interpretation emphasizes determination and independence; Bender's main Soul Urge interpretation adds the longing to make an individual contribution. Read together, they suggest a wish to act from your own convictions, rather than merely to look confident.

The difficulty can be turning that wish into a test you must pass alone. Advice may feel like interference, or a modest beginning may seem inadequate because it does not yet distinguish you. At the other extreme, waiting to feel certain can leave the desired independence largely unexpressed. These are possible tensions within the interpretation, not evidence that the name has created a particular behavior.

As an editorial application, choose one decision you can genuinely own and define what a reasonable first attempt would involve. Accept assistance without handing over the decision itself. Notice whether the result feels meaningful to you even when nobody applauds it. Everyday fulfillment can include both personal authorship and freely chosen cooperation.
