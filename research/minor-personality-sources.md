# Minor Personality — research and synthesis notes

Accessed **November 13, 2026 (America/Vancouver)**. Research was performed live for this topic over HTTPS. The relevant content of every cited article and the identified sections of both public PDF samples were read; search snippets were used only for discovery.

## Scope and strength of evidence

Four publisher families contribute substantive material, with different roles: Hans Decoz / World Numerology provides the explicit Minor definition and direct result material; Numerology.com supplies the main Personality definition and general number symbolism; Felicia Bender supplies an adjacent-position name-scope contrast; Sarah Yip supplies a current-legal-name terminology contrast. This is not four independent Minor Personality series. The 29 citations are pages/documents, not 29 source families.

Decoz’s Personality pages contain explicit Minor FAQs for 1–4, 6–9, 11, 22 and 33. The general definition in those FAQs uses the short name introduced socially and describes a casual impression. Two live public samples, S1 and S8, explicitly derive Minor Personality from current-name consonants and emphasize how one thinks or hopes others perceive one. The consulted D5 page contains main Personality material only. No second publisher’s full Minor-specific series was found within the accessible research destinations. The network policy allows the five starting-site families, so this was a bounded search, not a claim about the whole web.

Each result compares at least two relevant interpretations: Minor/main Personality material from Decoz and general numeric symbolism from Numerology.com. The latter is explicitly supplemental, not a second Minor-specific interpretation. The short FAQs and sample passages belong to the same Decoz family and are not counted as independent confirmation. The main Personality text and its Minor FAQ are distinguished even when they share themes.

## Valid range and chosen project convention

The guide retains 1–9, 11/2, 22/4, 33/6 for this name-number procedure. D0 preserves 11/22/33 for Personality, and D11/D22/D33 explicitly discuss the corresponding Minor results. This is a sourced convention, not a claim that every school retains masters in every minor position. Roots 2/4/6 are shown, without ranking people.

The project rule is current first/middle/last inputs; if no alternative is supplied, birth-name inputs are reused. Within each whitespace-separated word, punctuation is removed, only consonants are added, and the total is reduced while retaining 11/22/33. Reduced word totals are added and reduced by the same rule. A/E/I/O/U are vowels; Y and W are consonants. Accent decomposition and A–Z restrictions come from the existing calculator; no universal non-Latin transliteration is claimed.

A word without consonants contributes 0, but a complete name without consonants creates no interpretable result under the selected method. The existing calculator returns 0 for that case. There is no source-supported Minor Personality 0 description in this guide, and it is not a thirteenth result. The structured data records it separately under non_interpreted_states. Zero does not mean someone has no personality.

## Calculation checks and consequential alternatives

- **Current versus short name:** D1–D4 and other Minor FAQs say short/social introduction name. DN and the samples say current name. The project can retain a current middle name that the social introduction omits. Record the exact name rather than silently choosing one.
- **Bender scope:** BM calls minor names current first and last in an Expression calculation article. It supports a name-selection contrast; it does not provide a Minor Personality formula or result meanings.
- **Yip terminology:** Y describes a current legal name as Destiny, not Minor Personality. It illustrates a different naming scope and label; no consonant-only formula is inferred from it. Its embedded video and linked case studies were not used.
- **Y:** D0 allows Y to function as vowel or consonant by context. The project always includes it among consonants. Invented Bobby Smith gives Bobby 2+2+2+7=13→4 and Smith 1+4+2+8=15→6; 4+6=10→1. Excluding Y would give 6+6=12→3.
- **Name-part reduction:** D0 rejects one continuous raw consonant sum. The project reduces words before combining them. Completed word/final totals, not arbitrary partial additions, determine master retention.
- **Birth comparison:** Invented Robert James Smith has consonant word results Robert 22, James 6, Smith 6; total 34→7. With current Bobby Smith, main Personality is 7 and Minor Personality is 1. Without an alternative name, the project gives equal main/minor results because the input is the same.
- **Master feasibility:** The existing nameNumbers function returns 11 for Kevin (K2+V4+N5), 22 for Robert (R9+B2+R9+T2), and 33 for invented Kevin Kevin Kevin (11+11+11). These are calculation fixtures, not personal readings.
- **No timed effects:** DN proposes a gradual name-change effect; NM describes difficult or burdensome development. The guide does not adopt either as a factual prediction or a required experience.

## Writing and attribution boundaries

Profiles are original current-name syntheses, not copied or retitled versions of the earlier main Personality PDF. Each has three connected paragraphs; the final paragraph is practical editorial guidance. Titles and examples are editorial. The first two paragraphs also contain explicitly identified interpretive applications where sources do not directly discuss a specific social situation. Source IDs and source_roles record which evidence supports which profile; the PDF includes a short evidence note on each result page.

General numeric material supplements rather than silently replaces position-specific coverage. The whole current-name reading for 5 is an adaptation and is labeled in its first sentence, evidence note, structured data and source notes. For masters, the broader general symbolism is kept subordinate to the direct Minor description and its root. No number is used to assert physical appearance, health, trauma, mental-health conditions, psychic perception, guaranteed outcomes, required relationships, compulsory service, or deserved suffering.

## Discovery and access limitations

Bender’s live search for minor personality returned zero results; broader personality searches did not locate a direct Minor result series. Her minor search led to the Expression method article used as BM. Decoz’s live sitemap and page navigation led to the public sample PDFs and Personality articles. Numerology.com’s main-position definition was located in its Core Numbers article; an assumed standalone Personality URL returned 404. Its general number pages were reached through internal links. Sarah Yip’s written current-name example is methodological only. Ann Perry’s search endpoint returned a short challenge response with no usable article and was not counted. No source challenge, authentication, TLS verification, or network policy was bypassed. Full webpage and PDF caches are kept outside the checkout; only original notes, links and metadata are preserved here.
