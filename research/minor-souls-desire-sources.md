## Valid results before interpretation

The retained set is 1–9, 11/2, 22/4 and 33/6 under the chosen project method. D0 states that main and minor calculations are the same and explicitly preserves 11, 22 and 33 during name-part/final reduction. M’s eleven-result list stops at 22. Its omission of a 33 entry is a coverage gap, not by itself proof that D0’s method cannot yield 33 or that every school should reduce it. This guide includes 33 for project compatibility with a conspicuous adaptation label; it does not present a direct minor-33 interpretation as having been found.

Zero is not interpreted as a thirteenth result. The calculator can produce 0 when the entered name has no A/E/I/O/U. That state is stored separately in non_interpreted_states and means no vowel-derived result under the chosen rule, not an absence of motivation. A no-vowel word contributes 0 when other name parts do supply vowels.

## Selected project convention and consequential alternatives

- **Name:** current first/middle/last inputs, retaining a current middle name if entered. If no alternative is supplied, birth inputs are reused and main/minor values coincide; this is not two independent confirmations. D0 uses the social introduction name including surname, while M repeatedly says shortened name. BM, in an Expression article, calls minor names current first and last. BM is an adjacent-position naming comparison only.
- **Vowels:** A=1, E=5, I=9, O=6, U=3. Y and W are excluded to match app.js. DY uses conditional Y and explicitly names Lynn as vowel-Y; it does not assign W vowel status. B0 reports never/always/contextual Y approaches and occasional W variants, while excluding W in her practice. Those are Bender’s reports about other authors, not independently consulted books.
- **Reduction:** calculate vowel totals per whitespace-separated word, reduce by decimal digit sums while retaining 11/22/33, then combine and reduce by the same rule. Completed totals, not arbitrary partial sums, determine master retention. Punctuation has no numeric value. The calculator’s accent handling is a project implementation detail; document any chosen transliteration rather than claiming a universal non-Latin rule.
- **Birth/current arithmetic:** invented Robert James Smith has vowel word totals 11, 6 and 9; 26→8. Current Bobby Smith has 6 and 9; 15→6. Main Soul’s Desire can therefore be 8 while Minor Soul’s Desire is 6.
- **Conditional Y:** Lynn Ng yields 0 under the fixed rule; adding Y=7, as a vowel-Y convention does for Lynn, gives 7. This difference must not be silently hidden.
- **Reduction-order contrast:** invented John Amelia gives John 6 and Amelia 16→7; combined 13→4. The unreduced raw vowel total is 22. D0 explicitly rejects one continuous raw-total method.
- **Master feasibility:** the existing nameNumbers function yields 11 for invented Joe Ng, 22 for Joe Joe, and 33 for Joe Joe Joe. Joe contains O6+E5=11; Ng contributes 0. These are arithmetic fixtures, not claims about real people.
- **No software changes:** calculator source and behavior were inspected and exercised without modification. Its fixed Y/W rule and default name copying remain intact.

## Interpretive and editorial boundaries

M often describes a name as increasing intelligence, skills, confidence, or creative powers. The guide does not assert causal name effects or established changes in ability. It treats these claims as symbolism, compares them with wants and fulfillment in the main sources, and explains that reframing in the methodology. A desire is not proof of a capacity. Concrete everyday examples, titles, and practical final paragraphs are editorial applications, not claims directly tested by sources.

The profiles are freshly written about current-name motivational emphasis and are not retitled versions of the completed birth-name guide. Main Soul’s Desire remains the birth-name position in this project. Minor interpretations do not announce a newly activated life phase, displace the main number, or require a person to change their name.

Excluded source claims include innate or enhanced talent as fact, psychic ability, clinical conditions, addiction, compulsory marriage or parenthood, gendered domestic roles, guaranteed wealth, necessary hardship, deserved mistreatment, and age-specific spiritual awakenings. For 33 the root 6 remains central without implying that 33 outranks a person with 6.
